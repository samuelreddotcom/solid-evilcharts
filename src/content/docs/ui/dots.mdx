# Dots

`<Dot>` and `<ActiveDot>` nest inside a series element — a `<Line>`, an
`<Area>`, or a `<Radar>`. `<Dot>` is the resting marker; `<ActiveDot>` is the
one under the pointer.

```tsx
<EChartsLineChart.Line dataKey="desktop">
  <EChartsLineChart.Dot variant="border" />
  <EChartsLineChart.ActiveDot variant="colored-border" />
</EChartsLineChart.Line>
```

## Presence is the switch

<ApiTable>
  <ApiRow name="element omitted" type="variant">Resolves to `"none"` — no marker.</ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="element present, no variant" type="variant">Resolves to `"default"`.</ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="element present with variant" type="variant">That variant.</ApiRow>
</ApiTable>

## Variants

<ApiTable>
  <ApiRow name="none" type="variant">No marker drawn.</ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="default" type="variant">Solid dot in the series colour.</ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="border" type="variant">Series colour with a background-coloured ring.</ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="colored-border" type="variant">Inverted — background fill, series-coloured ring.</ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="ping" type="variant">A pulsing halo around the dot.</ApiRow>
</ApiTable>

<Callout title="`ping` uses its own alpha, deliberately">
  The halo is drawn at a flat 28% alpha regardless of the series colour's own.
  That is why `echarts-dot` has an alpha helper separate from the one in
  `chart-tokens`: this one **replaces** alpha, while the shared one
  **multiplies** it. They look like duplication and are not — a translucent
  series would otherwise produce an invisible halo.
</Callout>
